### A. STORYBRAND SB7 FRAMEWORK (Donald Miller)

The customer is the HERO. The brand is the GUIDE. Every piece of communication must follow this arc:

**1. CHARACTER (The Customer)**
- Define ONE thing the customer wants, connected to survival: conserving money, saving time, building networks, gaining status, accumulating resources, desire for meaning
- Open a "story gap" between where they are and where they want to be
- Test: can a stranger answer "what does this brand want for me?" in 5 seconds?

**2. PROBLEM (Three Layers)**
- **External Problem**: The tangible, surface-level challenge (need better analytics, faster deal cycles)
- **Internal Problem**: How it FEELS — frustration, self-doubt, embarrassment, incompetence, confusion. "Do I have what it takes?" THIS is why people actually buy.
- **Philosophical Problem**: What's inherently WRONG about the situation. Uses "ought" and "shouldn't." ("Partners shouldn't make IC decisions on incomplete data.")
- **The Villain**: Must be a root source (not a feeling), relatable, singular, and real. No fearmongering.

**3. GUIDE (Empathy + Authority)**
- Express empathy FIRST: "We understand how it feels to..."
- Demonstrate authority through: (a) testimonials (3 is ideal), (b) statistics, (c) awards/logos, (d) credentials
- Never position the brand as the hero. Customer = Luke. Brand = Yoda.

**4. PLAN (Process Plan + Agreement Plan)**
- Process Plan: 3-6 numbered steps showing the path. Name the plan.
- Agreement Plan: Counter every fear with an agreement ("No lock-in," "No follow-up unless you initiate")
- Purpose: reduce confusion and lower perceived risk

**5. CALL TO ACTION (Direct + Transitional)**
- Direct CTA: "Buy Now," "Start the Scorecard" — bold, prominent, repeated as user scrolls, different color, top-right AND center above fold
- Transitional CTA: Lower commitment — free PDF, webinar, sample, free trial
- Never be passive about the ask. Passivity signals lack of belief in the product.

**6. FAILURE (Stakes)**
- Show what happens if they DON'T act. Moderate fear — not too much (people block), not too little (no urgency).
- Loss aversion: people are 2-3× more motivated to avoid loss than achieve gain.
- Structure: (a) show vulnerability to threat, (b) state they should take action, (c) name a specific action, (d) challenge them to take it.

**7. SUCCESS (The Transformation)**
- Be SPECIFIC about the end state. Not vague. Kennedy: "put a man on the moon."
- Three endings: (a) Status/Power — access, scarcity, premium identity; (b) Completeness — reduced anxiety, reduced workload, more time; (c) Self-Realization — inspiration, acceptance, transcendence
- Show the identity TRANSFORMATION: "From [confused/overwhelmed] → To [competent/in-control]"

**Website Rules:**
- The Grunt Test: within 5 seconds a caveman must answer: (1) What do you offer? (2) How will it make my life better? (3) What do I need to do to buy it?
- Above the fold: offer + clear CTA + image of success
- Eye moves in Z pattern: top-left = logo, top-right = CTA button, center = offer + CTA
- "Pretty websites don't sell things. Words sell things."
- Most effective sites use ~10 sentences total on homepage
- Every word/image must come from the BrandScript. If it doesn't fit one of the 7 categories, it's noise.

**One-Liner Formula:**
[Character] + [Problem] + [Plan] + [Success]

### B. HORMOZI VALUE EQUATION ($100M Offers + $100M Leads)

**The Value Equation:**
```
Value = (Dream Outcome × Perceived Likelihood of Achievement) / (Time Delay × Effort & Sacrifice)
```

**To maximize value, optimize all four:**

**B1. Dream Outcome (↑ INCREASE)**
- What does the prospect actually want? Not the vehicle, the destination.
- Frame in terms of STATUS gained: "Your IC committee will see data they've never had."
- "Sell the vacation, not the plane flight."

**B2. Perceived Likelihood of Achievement (↑ INCREASE)**
- People pay for CERTAINTY. Increase through: social proof, track record, guarantees, specificity, case studies.
- A surgeon's 10,000th patient vs. first = same surgery, dramatically different perceived value.

**B3. Time Delay (↓ DECREASE)**
- Shorter = more valuable. "Fast beats free."
- Provide short-term wins that keep engagement while the long-term result builds.
- Liposuction ($25K, 1 afternoon) vs. bootcamp ($100/mo, 12+ months) = same outcome, 250× price difference.

**B4. Effort & Sacrifice (↓ DECREASE)**
- "Done for you" > "Done with you" > "Do it yourself" in pricing power.
- Eliminate steps, simplify, automate. Every extra step = lower perceived value.
- If effort/sacrifice = 0 and time delay = 0, perceived value → infinity.

**Grand Slam Offer Process:**
1. Identify the dream outcome
2. List EVERY problem/obstacle (use all 4 value drivers as categories)
3. Create a solution for each problem
4. Design delivery vehicles (1-on-1 / group / DIY vs DWY vs DFY / live vs recorded)
5. Trim high-cost/low-value items; stack remaining into named bundles with dollar values

**Guarantee Frameworks:**
- Unconditional (no questions asked)
- Conditional (if you do X and don't get Y, we Z)
- Anti-guarantee ("all sales final" with a compelling reason)
- Implied/performance-based (pay per result, rev-share)
- Stack guarantees for maximum risk reversal
- Name guarantees creatively for memorability

**Naming (M-A-G-I-C):**
- M = Magnetic reason why (event, season, milestone)
- A = Avatar (who is this for?)
- G = Goal (dream outcome, specific)
- I = Interval (time to achieve)
- C = Container word (Challenge, Blueprint, Bootcamp, System, Sprint)

**Scarcity (ethical, quantity-based):** Limited seats, limited bonuses, never available again
**Urgency (ethical, time-based):** Cohort deadlines, seasonal windows, pricing changes, exploding opportunities

**Bonus Stacking:**
- Value of bonuses should ECLIPSE core offer value
- Each bonus addresses a specific objection/obstacle
- Tools & checklists > additional training (lower effort = higher perceived value)
- Name each bonus with a benefit-driven title + assign dollar value

**The Core Four (Lead Generation):**
1. Warm Outreach (cheapest, easiest)
2. Content Creation (value per second)
3. Cold Outreach (direct contact)
4. Paid Advertising (scaling)
- Rule of 100: 100 outreaches/day OR 100 min content/day OR $100 ads/day

### C. CIALDINI'S 7 PRINCIPLES + PRE-SUASION

#### The 7 Principles of Influence:

**C1. Reciprocation**
- Give before asking. Free tools, insights, audits.
- Most powerful when gifts are: meaningful, unexpected, and customized.
- Rejection-then-Retreat (Door-in-the-Face): Start with large request, retreat to smaller = 3× more compliance + greater follow-through.

**C2. Commitment & Consistency**
- Foot-in-the-Door: small commitment → larger commitment
- Written > verbal. Public > private. Effortful > easy. Internally motivated > externally pressured.
- The Lowball: get commitment first, then reveal full terms. The person generates their own reasons to stay.
- Scorecard/assessment tools = powerful commitment device (written answers, time invested, identity statements)

**C3. Social Proof**
- We determine correctness by observing others. Most powerful under UNCERTAINTY and from SIMILAR others.
- "Fastest-growing," "most popular," peer testimonials, real-time activity signals
- Canned laughter effect: even obviously fake social proof works
- Salting the tip jar: seed initial social proof

**C4. Authority**
- Milgram: 65% delivered maximum shocks when ordered by authority
- Symbols: titles, clothes (design quality), trappings (professional assets)
- **Vulnerability-then-Strength**: Lead with a weakness → perceived as more honest → subsequent claims trusted more. Warren Buffett technique.

**C5. Liking**
- Physical attractiveness (halo effect), similarity, compliments, contact/familiarity, cooperation, positive association
- Mirror audience's language and concerns. Show founder story. Create shared-enemy narratives.
- Tupperware model: leverage existing relationships

**C6. Scarcity**
- Limited number, limited time, exclusive information
- NEWLY scarce > always scarce (revolutions happen when gains are taken away)
- Competition amplifies scarcity ("3 others viewing this now")
- Frame as LOSS: "Don't miss..." > "You could gain..."

**C7. Unity (The 7th Principle)**
- Strongest influence: not "like us" but "OF us." Shared identity, not shared preferences.
- Kinship language: "brothers," "family," "we are one"
- Co-creation: asking for advice creates investment in the outcome
- Synchronized action: moving/working together creates bonding

#### Pre-Suasion Techniques:

**Privileged Moments:**
- Channel attention BEFORE the message. What's focal = what's important.
- Positive Test Strategy: "Are you adventurous?" → person recalls adventure → more open to new offers
- Target Chuting: "Do you consider yourself someone who values X?" → 77% compliance boost

**Association & Priming:**
- Words prime behavior (hostile words → aggression; achievement words → performance)
- Metaphors channel thought: "crime as beast" → punitive; "crime as virus" → systemic reform
- Cognitive ease/fluency: easy-to-process = more liked, trusted, perceived as true

**Attention = Importance:**
- The Focusing Illusion: "Nothing is as important as you think it is while you are thinking about it."
- Background manipulation: cloud backgrounds → comfort priorities; coin backgrounds → price priorities
- What's FOCAL is presumed CAUSAL

**The Magnetizers (what HOLDS attention):**
- Self-relevant information ("you" not "people")
- The Unfinished (Zeigarnik Effect): incomplete → drives toward closure
- The Mysterious: open with a puzzle, resolve through content

**Matching Principles to Relationship Stage:**
- Cultivation: Reciprocation + Liking
- Uncertainty Reduction: Social Proof + Authority
- Motivating Action: Consistency + Scarcity

### D. KAHNEMAN — THINKING, FAST AND SLOW

**D1. System 1 vs System 2**
- System 1: automatic, fast, effortless, gullible, biased to believe. Designs should target System 1 first.
- System 2: effortful, slow, lazy, in charge of doubting but often endorses System 1.
- When System 2 is depleted (tired, overwhelmed), people believe almost anything.

**D2. Cognitive Ease / Fluency**
- Easy = familiar, true, good, effortless. Strained = vigilant, suspicious.
- Maximize legibility. Simple language. High contrast. Repetition makes things feel true.
- Rhyming aphorisms judged more insightful. Easy-to-pronounce names preferred.
- "Do NOT use complex language where simpler will do. Pretentious language = low credibility."

**D3. Anchoring**
- First number seen biases ALL subsequent judgments. Even random anchors work.
- Show highest-value package first. Display "original price" before discount.
- Use specific large numbers for impact ("€2,5M rendementsverlies" anchors higher than "significant loss").

**D4. Loss Aversion (Prospect Theory)**
- Losses loom 2× larger than equivalent gains.
- Frame as loss prevention: "What does inaction cost?" is 2× more motivating than "What do you gain?"
- Reference dependence: outcomes coded relative to current state, not absolute.

**D5. WYSIATI (What You See Is All There Is)**
- System 1 builds the best story from available info. Confidence depends on story QUALITY, not evidence QUANTITY.
- One-sided evidence → MORE confidence than balanced evidence.
- Tell ONE clear, coherent, consistent story. Fewer strong proof points > many weak ones.

**D6. Halo Effect**
- One positive trait colors perception of everything else. Order matters: positive traits first.
- First impression of website design creates a halo over all content.
- Lead with strongest credential, most impressive result.

**D7. Framing Effects**
- "90% survival rate" ≠ "10% mortality rate" psychologically
- Frame as gains, investment, what-you-get. Not losses, costs, what-you-give-up.
- Doctors equally susceptible — framing transcends expertise.

**D8. Peak-End Rule**
- Experiences judged by peak moment + ending. Duration neglected.
- End every interaction on a high note. Create a peak moment of insight/delight.
- Post-purchase experience shapes the ENTIRE memory of buying.

**D9. Endowment Effect**
- People value what they own 2-14× more. Once they "have" it, loss aversion kicks in.
- Free trials, personalized results, assessments → create ownership BEFORE purchase.

**D10. Status Quo Bias**
- Default = powerful nudge. Make desired action the default.
- Frame recommended option as the standard/normal choice.

**D11. Certainty Effect**
- Moving from 95% → 100% has disproportionate impact.
- "100% geld-terug garantie" is disproportionately more attractive than "zeer waarschijnlijk resultaat."
- Eliminate ALL perceived risk for maximum conversion.

### E. RORY SUTHERLAND — ALCHEMY

**E1. Psycho-Logic vs Logic**
- Logic gets you to the same place as competitors. Psycho-logic is the source of advantage.
- "Psychological moonshots": making a train journey 20% more ENJOYABLE costs 1% of making it 20% faster. Same with the Uber map.

**E2. The Four S's:**
- **Signalling**: Costly signals are credible (engagement rings, The Knowledge, marble in banks, expensive ads). Cheap signals can be faked. Brand investment = trust signal.
- **Subconscious Hacking**: Placebos work. Branded > generic. Higher price = higher perceived efficacy. Red Bull Strategy: making it "worse" (bad taste, small can, high price) made it MORE powerful because flaws signalled potency.
- **Satisficing**: People don't choose the best; they choose what they're most CERTAIN is good. Brands = "guarantee of non-crapness." Low variance > high average (McDonald's principle).
- **Psychophysics**: Perception ≠ reality. Context changes everything. The packaging IS the product. Words change taste ("low fat" makes food taste worse). A clean car DRIVES better in perception.

**E3. The Opposite of a Good Idea Can Also Be a Good Idea**
- Both extremes work; the middle fails. Luxury AND bargain thrive; mid-range dies.
- Long letter AND very short letter sell; medium letter fails.
- "Not many own this" AND "everyone has this" both work.

**E4. Costliness as Signal**
- Expensive advertising signals confidence and longevity. The ACT of advertising persuades beyond content.
- Marble, rope-handled bags, free limoncello = short-term costs that only pay off long-term = honest intent signals.
- Digital advertising loses this: targeted, measurable, cheap = loses the costly signalling dimension.

**E5. Friction as Feature (IKEA/Betty Crocker Effect)**
- Adding effort can INCREASE perceived value. "Just Add an Egg" outsold "Just Add Water."
- Self-assembly = ownership. Effort = value.
- Score: an assessment that requires YOUR answers feels more valuable than a generic report.

**E6. Reframing**
- "A change in perspective is worth 80 IQ points."
- Same facts, different context = different decision.
- JCPenney disaster: removed coupons/sales, lowered base price. Customers wanted the FEELING of a deal, not the lowest price.
- Discount = cheerful narrative. Low price = cheapskate feeling.

**E7. Satisficing & Trust**
- "People don't choose Brand A because they think it's better, but because they are more certain it is good."
- On eBay, 95% sellers get half the price of 100% sellers. We pay disproportionate premiums to eliminate small uncertainty.
- "No one ever got fired for buying IBM" = the power of safe choices in B2B.

**E8. Test Counterintuitive Things**
- "Test counterintuitive things, because no one else ever does."
- If an idea makes immediate logical sense, someone already tried it. The competitive advantage is in psycho-logic.

### F. NUDGE / BEHAVIORAL DESIGN (Thaler & Sunstein)

**F1. Choice Architecture**
- There is no neutral design. Every presentation influences outcomes.
- Primary principle: "If you want to encourage some action, Make It Easy."

**F2. Defaults**
- Most powerful nudge. What happens if someone does nothing?
- Automatic enrollment: participation jumps from ~50% to ~90%.
- Types: mass defaults, personalized defaults, smart defaults.

**F3. Reduce Friction (Anti-Sludge)**
- Remove EVERY unnecessary step between desire and action.
- Sludge audit: systematically find and eliminate friction points.
- Dark patterns = sludge used against the customer.

**F4. Social Norms**
- Informing people what others do changes behavior. Descriptive norms + injunctive norms (smiley face).
- Correct misperceived norms to unlock change.

**F5. Give Feedback**
- Show progress. Show results. Show comparison to peers.
- Warning systems must avoid crying wolf.

**F6. Expect Error**
- Design for mistakes. Post-completion errors. Make the system forgiving.
- Reduce options. Fewer choices = better decisions = higher satisfaction.

**F7. EAST Framework (Behavioral Insights Team):**
- **Easy**: reduce friction, simplify, default to desired action
- **Attractive**: make it salient, use images, personalize
- **Social**: show what others do, use norms, leverage networks
- **Timely**: prompt at the right moment, use deadlines, leverage transitions

### G. OBVIOUSLY AWESOME — POSITIONING (April Dunford)

**G1. The 5+1 Components:**
1. Competitive Alternatives (what would customers do without you?)
2. Unique Attributes (what can you do that alternatives can't?)
3. Value (what value do those attributes deliver?)
4. Target Customer Segments (who cares most about that value?)
5. Market Category (what frame makes your value obvious?)
6. (+1) Relevant Trend (what tailwind amplifies your positioning?)

**G2. Context Is Everything**
- Joshua Bell in the subway: world-class = ignored without context.
- Positioning = setting the scene so the audience can appreciate what follows.

**G3. Three Positioning Styles:**
- Head-to-Head (compete in existing category)
- Big Fish, Small Pond (dominate a subsegment)
- Create a New Category (highest risk/reward)

**G4. Competitive Alternatives ≠ Competitors**
- Ask: "What would customers DO if we didn't exist?" Often = spreadsheets, manual processes, nothing.

### H. CONTAGIOUS — STEPPS (Jonah Berger)

**H1. Social Currency** — People share what makes them look good.
- Inner remarkability (surprise, novelty)
- Game mechanics / achievement (visible status)
- Exclusivity / scarcity (insider feeling)

**H2. Triggers** — Top of mind = tip of tongue.
- Link product to frequent environmental cues. Cheerios > Disney World in word-of-mouth (breakfast = daily trigger).

**H3. Emotion** — When we care, we share.
- HIGH-arousal emotions drive sharing: awe, excitement, anger, anxiety.
- LOW-arousal emotions suppress sharing: contentment, sadness.

**H4. Public** — Built to show = built to grow.
- Make the private public. Leave behavioral residue. Self-advertising products.

**H5. Practical Value** — News you can use.
- Rule of 100: under €100 = percentage discount feels bigger; over €100 = absolute discount feels bigger.
- Package expertise into useful, shareable formats.

**H6. Stories** — Trojan Horse narratives.
- Brand must be INTEGRAL to the story. Test: can someone retell it without mentioning you?

### I. PREDICTABLY IRRATIONAL (Dan Ariely)

**I1. Decoy Effect**: Add clearly inferior option to make target option shine. The Economist pricing experiment.

**I2. Arbitrary Coherence**: First price creates permanent anchor. Break anchoring chains by creating experiences different enough that old price references don't apply.

**I3. The Power of FREE**: Zero is not just another price — it eliminates ALL perceived risk. Amazon France: 1 franc ≠ free. Even tiny charges create friction that zero removes entirely.

**I4. Social vs Market Norms**: Introducing market norms into social relationships DESTROYS them, possibly permanently. The daycare fine paradox. Build social-norm relationships for loyalty; never cross the streams.

**I5. Endowment + IKEA Effect**: People value what they own / built 2-14× more. Trials, personalization, and effort all create ownership.

**I6. Expectation Shapes Experience**: Brand expectations literally alter neural experience (Pepsi vs Coke brain scans). Price enhances perceived quality (placebo pricing). Set HIGH expectations through branding, presentation, and price.

**I7. Fewer Options = Better Decisions**: Paradox of choice. Reduce the decision set. Help customers by narrowing.

### J. SETH GODIN (This Is Marketing / Purple Cow / Tribes)

**J1. Smallest Viable Market**: Find the smallest group who would be MOST disappointed if you ceased to exist. Speak directly to their worldview.

**J2. "People Like Us Do Things Like This"**: Culture and identity drive decisions. Marketing = offering pathways to belong.

**J3. Tension**: Marketing creates and relieves tension. The gap between where they are and where they could be.

**J4. Permission Marketing**: Earn the right to continue the conversation. Don't interrupt; be invited.

**J5. Remarkability (Purple Cow)**: If it's not remarkable (worthy of remark), it's invisible. The only marketing that matters is word-of-mouth from people who experienced something worth talking about.

**J6. Status Roles**: Affiliation (belonging) vs. Dominion (power). Understand which your audience seeks.

**J7. The Five Steps**: Invent something worthwhile → Design for a tiny market → Tell a matching story → Spread the word → Show up consistently for years.

### K. VALUE PROPOSITION DESIGN (Osterwalder)

**K1. Customer Profile:**
- **Jobs**: Functional (tasks), Social (how they want to be perceived), Emotional (feelings they seek)
- **Pains**: Undesired outcomes, obstacles, risks. "What keeps them awake at night?"
- **Gains**: Required, expected, desired, unexpected. "What would make them a hero?"

**K2. Value Map:**
- Products & Services → Pain Relievers → Gain Creators
- Achieve FIT when value map addresses the most important jobs, worst pains, essential gains.

**K3. Three Types of Fit:**
1. Problem-Solution Fit (on paper)
2. Product-Market Fit (in the market — traction)
3. Business Model Fit (in the bank — profitable)

**K4. B2B Stakeholder Unbundling:**
- Create separate profiles for: Influencers, Recommenders, Economic Buyers, Decision Makers, End Users, Saboteurs.

## PART 2: THE SYNTHESIZED AUDIT CHECKLIST

Apply these checks to EVERY element:

### MACRO-LEVEL (Full Page / Site)

- [ ] **StoryBrand Arc**: Does the full page follow Character → Problem → Guide → Plan → CTA → Failure → Success?
- [ ] **Grunt Test**: Can a stranger answer the 3 questions in 5 seconds? (What? How better? What to do?)
- [ ] **Value Equation**: Does each page maximize Dream Outcome × Likelihood while minimizing Time Delay × Effort?
- [ ] **One Coherent Story (WYSIATI)**: Is there ONE clear narrative or are there competing messages?
- [ ] **Positioning (Dunford)**: Is the market category clear? Would customers know what to compare this to?
- [ ] **Smallest Viable Market (Godin)**: Is the messaging specific to one audience, or generic?
- [ ] **Peak-End Rule**: Does the page end on a high note / strong CTA?
- [ ] **Halo Effect**: Does the strongest credential/result lead?

### HERO / ABOVE THE FOLD

- [ ] **Z-Pattern**: Logo top-left, CTA top-right, offer + CTA center?
- [ ] **Heading**: States dream outcome OR solves a problem OR makes an aspirational identity promise?
- [ ] **Internal Problem**: Does the copy address how the customer FEELS, not just their external problem?
- [ ] **Eyebrow**: Pre-frames the audience (Pre-suasion target chuting)?
- [ ] **Sub-CTA Friction Reduction**: "No account," "12 minutes," "Free," "No obligation" (Hormozi: reduce effort/time)?
- [ ] **Cognitive Ease**: Simple words, high contrast, legible font, no jargon?
- [ ] **Anchoring**: Is the first number/stat a strong anchor?

### PAIN / PROBLEM SECTIONS

- [ ] **Three Layers**: External problem + Internal problem (feeling) + Philosophical problem (what's wrong)?
- [ ] **Single Villain**: Is there one clear enemy, not multiple?
- [ ] **Loss Framing**: Framed as what they LOSE, not what they could gain (2× more motivating)?
- [ ] **Availability**: Are pains vivid, concrete, specific (not abstract)?
- [ ] **Pre-suasion Questions**: Are pain points framed as questions that force self-assessment?

### GUIDE / TRUST SECTIONS

- [ ] **Empathy First, Authority Second**: "We understand..." before credentials?
- [ ] **Vulnerability-then-Strength (Cialdini)**: Lead with an honest limitation, then pivot to strengths?
- [ ] **Social Proof**: Testimonials from SIMILAR others (same industry, size, problem)?
- [ ] **Authority Symbols**: Logos, credentials, media mentions, statistics?
- [ ] **Unity (Cialdini 7th)**: "Of us" language? Shared identity? Kinship framing?
- [ ] **Costly Signalling (Sutherland)**: Does the design/presentation signal quality investment?

### PLAN / STEPS SECTIONS

- [ ] **3-6 Steps Maximum**: Numbered, named?
- [ ] **Effort Reduction (Hormozi)**: Does each step feel EASY? "Multiple choice," "12 minutes," "No open fields"?
- [ ] **Time Compression (Hormozi)**: Is speed/immediacy emphasized? "Direct na afronding"?
- [ ] **Agreement Plan**: Are fears countered with explicit agreements?
- [ ] **Default to Desired Action (Nudge)**: Is the recommended path the obvious/default one?

### CTA SECTIONS

- [ ] **Direct CTA**: Bold, different color, repeated as user scrolls, in top-right AND center?
- [ ] **Transitional CTA**: Lower-commitment alternative next to the direct CTA?
- [ ] **Specificity**: Does the CTA tell them exactly what they GET? ("Start de Scorecard" > "Begin")
- [ ] **Risk Reversal**: Is there a guarantee, "no obligation," or "free" element?
- [ ] **Scarcity/Urgency (Cialdini)**: Time-bound, limited spots, exclusive access?
- [ ] **Loss Frame**: "Don't miss..." / "Voorkom dat..." rather than "Discover..." / "Ontdek..."?
- [ ] **FREE Power (Ariely)**: Can any element be made literally free to eliminate all risk?

### FAILURE / STAKES SECTIONS

- [ ] **Present**: Is there a failure section at all? (Most sites skip this.)
- [ ] **Moderate Fear**: Not too much (blocked), not too little (no urgency)?
- [ ] **Quantified**: Is the cost of inaction made specific? ("0,5× EBITDA = €2,5M")?
- [ ] **Four-Step Fear Appeal**: Vulnerable → Should act → Specific action → Challenge to act?

### SUCCESS / TRANSFORMATION SECTIONS

- [ ] **Specific End State**: Vivid, concrete description of life after? Not vague?
- [ ] **Identity Transformation**: "From [X] → To [Y]"?
- [ ] **Status**: Does success increase the customer's perceived status among peers?
- [ ] **Images of Success**: Happy people engaging with the product/result?

### SOCIAL PROOF

- [ ] **Peer Similarity**: Testimonials from people LIKE the target audience?
- [ ] **Specificity**: Concrete results, numbers, named outcomes — not generic praise?
- [ ] **Three testimonials** (StoryBrand sweet spot)?
- [ ] **Transformation Arc**: Before state → frustration → discovery → result → life now?
- [ ] **Real-Time Activity**: "X others have completed..." / "X people viewing" (if applicable)?

### PRICING / INVESTMENT SECTIONS

- [ ] **Anchor High**: Most expensive / most valuable shown FIRST?
- [ ] **Decoy (Ariely)**: Is there a clearly inferior middle option that makes the target option shine?
- [ ] **Rule of 100 (Berger)**: Under €100 = percentage; over €100 = absolute amount?
- [ ] **Reframe as Investment**: "Investering," not "kosten"?
- [ ] **Placebo Pricing (Ariely/Sutherland)**: Does the price SIGNAL quality? Would lowering price reduce perceived value?
- [ ] **Value Stack (Hormozi)**: Are deliverables broken into named components with individual values that total more than the price?

### FAQ / OBJECTION HANDLING

- [ ] **Every Fear Addressed**: List all fears, counter each one?
- [ ] **Reciprocal Concessions**: If someone says no, is there a smaller ask?
- [ ] **Honest / Transparent Tone**: Vulnerability-then-strength?
- [ ] **Social Norms (not Market Norms)**: Does the language feel like a trusted advisor, not a salesperson?

### MICRO-LEVEL (Every Word)

- [ ] **Cognitive Ease**: Flesch-Kincaid ≤ 10. No compound sentences where simple ones work.
- [ ] **Active Voice**: Subject-verb-object. "Wij maken het zichtbaar" not "Het wordt zichtbaar gemaakt."
- [ ] **"You" not "We"**: Customer-centric framing. Count instances of "wij/ons" vs "u/uw."
- [ ] **Concrete > Abstract**: "€2,5M rendementsverlies" not "significant rendementsverlies."
- [ ] **Sensory Language**: Can the reader SEE, FEEL, or EXPERIENCE the outcome?
- [ ] **Rhyme/Rhythm**: Memorable phrases? Alliteration? Parallel structure?
- [ ] **No Jargon Without Translation**: Every technical term immediately explained in plain language.

### DESIGN / UX SIGNALS

- [ ] **Costly Signalling (Sutherland)**: Does the visual design signal investment and care?
- [ ] **Cognitive Ease (Kahneman)**: Clean layout, high contrast, ample whitespace, clear hierarchy?
- [ ] **Progress Indicators (Nudge)**: For multi-step processes, is progress visible?
- [ ] **Default Paths (Nudge)**: Is the recommended action the most visually prominent?
- [ ] **Endowment Creation**: Does any element create a sense of ownership before purchase (personalized results, "your" language)?
- [ ] **Friction Audit (Nudge)**: Count clicks/steps from landing to conversion. Can any be removed?
- [ ] **Mobile Friction**: Is every CTA thumb-reachable? Are tap targets large enough?
- [ ] **Satisficing (Sutherland)**: Does the design feel SAFE and RELIABLE, not just attractive?

### For a complete site audit:

### For a single page:

1. Feed this prompt + the page source
2. Ask: "Audit this page against all frameworks. For every element, identify the strongest applicable improvement. Quote current → proposed replacement. Cite the framework."

### For a copy pass:

1. Feed this prompt + all copy text
2. Ask: "Rewrite every heading, body paragraph, and CTA applying the synthesized frameworks. For each change, note which principle drives it."

### Priority order for maximum impact:

1. Hero/above-the-fold (first impression = halo effect over everything)
2. CTA clarity and repetition (if they don't know what to do, nothing else matters)
3. Pain section + internal problem (the real purchase motivator)
4. Failure/stakes section (loss aversion = 2× gain motivation)
5. Social proof specificity (peer similarity + concrete numbers)
6. Friction reduction (every removed step = higher conversion)
7. Everything else
